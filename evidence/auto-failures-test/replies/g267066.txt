```markdown
# Invoice Entry Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Total | The entered total matches the invoice digit for digit. Check the decimal point, the thousands separator and the number of digits. Where the invoice shows a subtotal and tax, check that they add up to the total entered. | / 5 |
| Invoice number | The number matches the invoice exactly, including letters, dashes and leading zeros. | / 5 |
| Supplier | The supplier named is the one that issued the invoice, not a parent company, a customer name or a similar-looking supplier already in the system. | / 5 |
| Due date | The date is the one printed on the invoice, read the right way round (14/11 is not 11/14). If the invoice gives terms (such as "30 days") and no date, the output says it worked the date out and shows how. | / 5 |
| Unclear fields | Where a scan is cut off, blurred or ambiguous, the output says so and asks for a check of the original. It does not guess. | / 5 |
| Missing information | The output notes what the invoice does not show, such as a purchase order number, a due date or a currency. | / 5 |
| Printed versus assumed | Anything not printed on the invoice (currency, due date from terms, which total is meant if there are several) is marked as an assumption. | / 5 |
| Left for approval | The entry is prepared for finance to approve. It is not marked approved, scheduled or paid, and it does not claim anyone has checked it. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- Enters a total, invoice number or due date that differs from the invoice without flagging the difference (the 14,180 entered as 1,418 case)
- Fills in an unreadable or missing figure with a guess and presents it as fact
- Enters the wrong supplier, so a payment would go to the wrong party
- Marks an entry as approved, paid or ready to pay without a person in finance having approved it

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt
```

Some notes on how I built it:

- **Why a wrong total is an automatic failure.** If it were only a low score, an output with a wrong total but a perfect supplier, number and date would still average well. Your 1,418 payment is exactly that case.
- **Why the total check includes the subtotal and tax.** A total that doesn't equal subtotal plus tax is a quick way to catch a dropped digit.
- **Your good examples.** They became the "Missing information" and "Unclear fields" areas.
- **My guesses.** The wrong-supplier failure and the "printed versus assumed" area come from my reading of your task, not from your examples. Drop either if it doesn't match how your invoices go wrong.

Before you trust it, score ten to fifteen real invoices with it, including the 14,180 one and a couple of ugly scans. Check that the scores match what a careful person in finance would conclude. If one area never scores below 5, or an area is missing, revise it then.

One process point. The 1,418 slipped through because nobody noticed it. Check whether the person approving sees only the entered figures or the original invoice next to them. A rubric catches what the AI gets wrong, but only if someone compares the entry against the original.
