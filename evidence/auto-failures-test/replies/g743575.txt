I'm not going to build a rubric for this one, and the reason comes from your own answers.

The instructions say to stop when the task has no real stakes if it goes wrong, and when you can't describe what a bad output looks like. Both apply here. You said a missed failure costs nothing, and you have no bad example because nothing has gone wrong. A rubric built on that would be guessed, not grounded, and scoring a quiz-round name on five-point scales across several areas would be more work than the names are worth.

"Name That Tune, Nearly" and "Capital Ideas" already work. If a name is poor, you will see it as soon as you read it.

Two things would change my answer:

- If a name ever does cause trouble, for example one that offended someone, gave away an answer or clashed with another round, tell me what happened. That gives us a real bad example, and I can build a short rubric from it.
- If you want a light check now, you don't need a rubric. Ask the tool for five names and pick the one you like best. If you want a guard against repeats, keep a list of past round names and tell the tool not to reuse them.

If the quiz is part of something with higher stakes, such as a client event or a competition with prizes, say so and we can look again.
