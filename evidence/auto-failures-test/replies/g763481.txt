# Tenancy Agreement Summary Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Accuracy against the agreement | Every term in the summary (rent, due date, deposit, notice) matches what the agreement says. Check each one against the text. | / 5 |
| Conditions kept | Conditions survived. "Increases only with two months' written notice" must not shrink to "rent can increase". | / 5 |
| Missing terms flagged | Where the agreement is silent (like the deposit scheme in your second good example), the summary says so and says to check before signing. | / 5 |
| Fact versus assumption | The summary separates what the agreement states from what the tool assumes or infers. Nothing assumed is written as fact. | / 5 |
| No invented protection | The summary does not say a term, scheme or safeguard exists unless the agreement names it. | / 5 |
| Ready for the landlord to check | Each key term can be checked quickly, ideally by quoting the agreement's own words. The summary leaves the signing decision to the landlord. | / 5 |

Six areas, well under the limit of ten.

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- States that the deposit is protected, or protected in a named or approved scheme, when the agreement names no scheme. This is your bad example.
- States any other deposit-protection detail as fact (a scheme name, a protection date or a deadline) when the agreement does not give it. This is the same mistake in a nearby field.

A person has to apply this rubric. It does not score itself. Run it on a handful of real summaries first, including at least one you already know is wrong.
