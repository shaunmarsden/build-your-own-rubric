The payroll ticket tells me the main lesson. The ticket gave a deadline ("pay run is at noon") and the tool ignored it. Marking a ticket too low costs far more than marking it too high, so the rubric treats those two mistakes differently.

## Draft rubric: AI ticket priority (P1 to P4)

### Step 1: Check for automatic failures first

If any of these happen, the output fails. Do not score it.

1. The ticket mentions a deadline within the next working day, or pay, payroll or payments to staff or customers, and the tool ranked it below P2.
2. The tool was unsure (it said so, or the ticket is vague) and still ranked the ticket P3 or P4 instead of passing it to a person.
3. The reason names a deadline, number of people or system that is not in the ticket.
4. No reason was given.
5. Salary figures or other personal details appear in the reason.

Your payroll example fails on number 1.

### Step 2: Score each area from 1 to 5

Use the same scale for every area:

- 1 = wrong, would cause harm
- 2 = weak, a person must redo it
- 3 = useful, but a person must check it
- 4 = strong, minor fix
- 5 = right, no change

| # | Area | What a 5 looks like |
|---|------|---------------------|
| 1 | Deadline caught | Every time limit in the ticket is noticed and used ("by noon", "before the audit") |
| 2 | Impact read correctly | It sees who is affected, how many, and whether work or pay stops |
| 3 | Matches our definitions | The priority fits the written P1 to P4 rules, not the tool's own idea of urgent |
| 4 | Reason can be checked | One line that points to words in the ticket |
| 5 | Facts apart from guesses | It says what the ticket states and what it is inferring |
| 6 | Missing information flagged | If the ticket lacks a deadline or a head count, it says so and routes to a person |
| 7 | No invented urgency | A P1 is backed by the ticket's own words, not by the user's tone |
| 8 | Sensitive details left out | The reason stays free of personal or payroll detail |

Total out of 40. Under 24 means do not trust the tool for that kind of ticket.

I left out tone and "action prepared for approval". If the tool only sets a priority, they do not apply. If it also pages the on-call engineer on its own, tell me and I will add a ninth area.

### Step 3: Record for each ticket

- Total score, and any automatic failure
- Which way the miss went (too low or too high). Count these separately.
- The one correction a person made
- What to change in the prompt before the next attempt

## Before you trust it

This draft is untested. Run it on about 15 to 20 past tickets, including the payroll one and a few the tool got right. Score them yourself, then check whether the rubric's verdict matches what a careful person on your desk would say. If it passes a ticket your team would call a miss, or fails one they would call fine, change the rubric. After a few weeks of real use, drop any area that nobody ever scores low.
