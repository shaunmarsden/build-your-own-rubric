## Allergen statement rubric

**Task:** An AI tool drafts the allergen statement for each product label from the recipe sheet. A person checks it before it is printed.

**How to use it:** Score each area from 1 to 5, using the scale below. Then check the automatic failures. An automatic failure ends the review, whatever the scores. A statement passes only if there is no automatic failure and no area scores below 4. The total is for comparing prompts or tools, not for passing a statement.

### The scale (the same for every area)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Unusable. It would cause harm if printed. |
| 2 | Weak. A serious problem a reviewer might miss. |
| 3 | Useful with review. The reviewer has to fix real things. |
| 4 | Strong with minor fixes. Wording or format only. |
| 5 | Ready to use. Nothing to change. |

### The scoring areas

1. **Every allergen on the recipe sheet is listed.** Check each ingredient on the sheet against your allergen list and make sure each one appears. A 5 means nothing is missing and nothing is reworded in a way that changes the meaning.
2. **Vague ingredients are flagged, not guessed.** Look for "seed mix", "spice blend", "chocolate", "flavouring", "margarine", "flour improver" or similar. Each one needs a note saying what could be hiding in it and what must be checked. A 5 means every such item is flagged. A 1 means the tool passed over one silently. This is the area that would have caught the sesame miss.
3. **Confirmed facts are kept apart from assumptions.** The statement should show what the sheet states, what the tool inferred, and what it doesn't know. A 5 means a reader can tell which is which at a glance.
4. **No "free from" claims without a source.** "No nuts" or "no soya" is only acceptable if the sheet or a supplier sheet says so. Silence on the sheet means "not stated", not "absent".
5. **Detail survives.** Name the specific cereal (wheat, oats, rye) and the specific nut, as your local rules require. Keep conditions such as "contains milk in the glaze only". A 5 means nothing is flattened into "contains cereals" or "contains nuts".
6. **Cross-contact wording is only what you gave it.** "May contain" lines should come from your own policy or the sheet. They should not be invented, and they should not be dropped.
7. **Wording and format fit the label.** The language is plain enough for a customer, allergens are emphasised the way you emphasise them on every label, and nothing irrelevant is included.
8. **It is prepared for approval, not presented as final.** It should be clearly marked as a draft and say what a person must check before printing. Your good example 2 ("Check the supplier sheet before printing") is a 5.

### Automatic failures

Any one of these fails the statement, whatever it scores:

- An allergen that is on the recipe sheet is missing from the statement.
- A vague or composite ingredient is passed over with no flag, as in the seeded loaf.
- The statement says or implies a product is free from an allergen when no source confirms it.
- It claims a check that never happened, such as "confirmed with supplier".
- It is presented as ready to print with no human check.

I'd keep this list short and keep it for harm. Over-declaring an allergen is annoying but safe, so I've left it as a scored area (7), not an automatic failure.

### What to record for each statement

- The total score and the lowest-scoring area
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction the human made
- What has to change in the prompt or the recipe sheet before the next draft

## Test it before you trust it

I haven't tested this against any real outputs, so run these steps before you rely on it:

1. Score about ten past statements. Include the seeded loaf, which should hit an automatic failure on the vague-ingredient rule, and your two good examples, which should pass.
2. Have the person who normally checks your labels score the same ten on their own. If your scores and theirs disagree, fix the wording of the area they disagreed on.
3. Revise it after real use. Drop an area nobody ever scores low, and add one if a new kind of miss turns up.
