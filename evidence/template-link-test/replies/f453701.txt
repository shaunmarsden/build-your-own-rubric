## Rubric: AI summaries of supplier contract clauses

**Use it:** every time a summary is about to go to procurement before signing. Score it against the clause text itself, never against your memory of the contract.

### Scoring areas (score each 1 to 5)

1. **Quoted figures match the clause.** Notice periods, durations, dates, fees, caps. The summary should use the exact figure from the clause, as in "90 days", not "about three months".
2. **Renewal and exit mechanics are right.** Whether the contract renews automatically, for how long, how much notice stops it, and what counts as valid notice.
3. **Conditions and caveats survived.** "Unless", "subject to", "except", carve-outs and caps all made it into the summary. A summary that keeps the rule and drops the condition fails here.
4. **Confirmed vs interpreted.** The summary makes clear what the clause says and what is the AI's reading of it.
5. **Missing information is named.** Clauses it was not given, schedules it did not see, cross-references it could not follow, and defined terms it could not check. It says "I have not seen X" instead of staying silent.
6. **Money and liability exposure is surfaced.** Liability caps, penalties, termination fees and price-change rights are shown with their figures, so procurement can see what a mistake would cost.
7. **Action is prepared, not declared done.** Deadlines are flagged for a person to diarise and unclear points go to legal. The summary never says the contract is fine, safe or approved.

Seven areas keeps it short enough to fill in during a normal sign-off.

### Scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Unusable. Wrong or misleading, and would cause harm if relied on. |
| 2 | Weak. Gets the main point but has a material gap or error. |
| 3 | Useful with review. Mostly right, but a person must check it line by line. |
| 4 | Strong with minor fixes. Accurate; small wording or detail changes only. |
| 5 | Ready to use. Accurate against the clause, caveats intact, gaps named. |

Record the lowest area score as well as the total. A total can hide one disastrous area, and your bad example was one wrong line in an otherwise reasonable summary. A suggested starting rule: ready to use only if no area is below 4 on areas 1 to 3. Adjust that once you have used it.

### Automatic failures

Any one of these fails the output, whatever the score:

- It states the opposite of the clause on renewal, notice, termination or liability (for example "no automatic renewal" when the clause renews for another year unless you give 60 days' notice).
- It gives a notice period, date, fee or cap that is not in the clause.
- It says a right, penalty or renewal is absent when it has not seen the relevant clause. Not seeing a term is not the same as the term not existing.
- It leaves out an automatic renewal or notice deadline that is in the text it was given.
- It says or implies the contract is cleared, safe to sign or legally reviewed.

### What to record for each summary

- Total score and lowest area score
- Any automatic failure, and which one
- The single most important correction a person made
- What to change in the prompt or process before the next run
