# Allergen Statement Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Every named allergen is listed | Take each ingredient on the recipe sheet, one at a time. Every allergen it names or clearly contains is in the statement (as in the granola bar: oats, nuts, milk). Check compound ingredients too, such as chocolate, pastry, or a bought-in mix. | / 5 |
| Vague ingredients are not passed as safe | Any ingredient that could hide an allergen ("seed mix", "spice blend", "flavouring", "chocolate", "coating") is either broken down from the supplier sheet or flagged. It is never silently left out. This is the seeded loaf failure. | / 5 |
| Missing information is flagged | Where the sheet does not say, the statement says so and names what to check and where, as in your soya example ("Check the supplier sheet before printing"). | / 5 |
| Confirmed facts are separate from assumptions | You can tell at a glance which allergens come straight from the recipe sheet and which are inferred or unknown. An inference is never written as if the sheet said it. | / 5 |
| Nothing invented | No allergen added that the sheet does not support, no "free from" or "suitable for" claim, and no "may contain" or cross-contact wording unless the sheet or you supplied it. Raise the cross-contact question; do not answer it. | / 5 |
| Matches the recipe sheet in front of it | Right product, right version of the sheet. Wording and quantities agree with it. Nothing carried over from a similar product. | / 5 |
| Fits the label | Plain wording a customer can read. Follows your label format and the legal allergen list for your market. Allergens are named the way the label requires. | / 5 |
| Prepared for approval, not treated as done | The output is a draft for a named person to check against the supplier sheets. It does not say "approved", "checked" or "ready to print" on its own authority. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- Leaves out an allergen that the recipe sheet names or that a listed ingredient contains, or treats a vague ingredient ("seed mix") as having no allergens.
- Fills a gap in the recipe sheet with a guess, in either direction, without saying it is a guess.
- States or implies that the product is free from an allergen without a source on the sheet.
- Says or implies the statement is approved, checked or safe to print, with no human sign-off.
- Adds an ingredient, supplier detail or allergen that is not on the recipe sheet.

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt

Three notes before you use it.

1. Test it before you trust it. Run the seeded loaf recipe sheet (the one that said "seed mix") through your AI tool again and score the result. It should hit the first automatic failure if sesame is missing and not flagged. If the rubric lets that output pass, it is wrong. Then score your two good examples. They should come out at 4 or 5. Then try three or four more real sheets and check that the scores match what a careful person on your team would say.
2. Fix the cause upstream too. A rubric catches the "seed mix" problem after the fact. The cheaper cure is to stop recipe sheets saying "seed mix" at all, or to have the tool refuse to draft until each vague ingredient has a supplier breakdown. Put that in your "what would need to change" record whenever it comes up.
3. A person has to apply it. The rubric does not make a statement safe. Someone who knows the recipe and has the supplier sheets must sign off every statement before it is printed, and the rubric only helps them do that the same way each time.
