```markdown
# Tenancy Agreement Summary Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Accuracy of key terms | Rent, due date, term length, deposit amount and notice periods match the agreement exactly. Good example: "rent 950 a month, due on the 1st". | / 5 |
| Deposit and scheme | The summary says only what the agreement says about the deposit. If a scheme is named, it is named. If none is named, the summary says so and tells the landlord to check before signing. | / 5 |
| Conditions and caveats | Conditions survived. Good example: rent "increases only with two months' written notice", not just "rent can increase". | / 5 |
| Confirmed vs assumed | Anything the agreement states is kept apart from anything the tool is inferring or assuming. | / 5 |
| Missing information | Terms the agreement leaves out are listed as missing, not filled in with a typical default. | / 5 |
| Fit for the reader | Plain language that a small landlord with no legal training can check against the document. | / 5 |
| Relevance and sensitivity | Only the key terms the landlord needs. Personal details of the tenant or others are not repeated without need. | / 5 |
| No invented authority | The summary does not say the agreement is "safe to sign", "compliant" or "legally fine", and does not promise anything the document does not. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- States that the deposit is protected, or protected in an approved scheme, when the agreement names no scheme
- States any other deposit-related fact (the amount, when it must be protected, how it is returned) that the agreement does not contain or that contradicts it

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt
```

Notes on how I built it:

- I kept the automatic failures to what your bad example showed and the same mistake in a nearby field (other deposit facts). I did not add others, such as missing a break clause. If you think another outcome should fail automatically, tell me whether it has ever happened. Otherwise it is better as a scored area.
- "Says it is safe to sign" sits in a scored area, not as an automatic failure, for that reason.
- Before you trust this for anything with real stakes, run it on a handful of real summaries, including ones where you already know the right answer, and check that the scores match what a careful person would conclude. In particular, check that a summary with the exact bad-example mistake gets caught.
- Revise it after real use. If nobody ever scores an area low, or you keep finding a problem it does not cover, change it.
- This is a check on the summary, not legal advice. For anything about the legal time limit or penalties, the landlord should confirm with a qualified person.
