# Applicant First-Reply Draft Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Facts match what is real | Every date, time, timeline and next step in the draft matches what the team has actually agreed. The "two weeks" timeline is fine because it is the real one. | / 5 |
| Confirmed versus not yet decided | The draft says plainly what is settled and what is not. It does not turn a hope or a plan into a fact. | / 5 |
| Missing information named | Where the tool lacks something, such as interview dates, it says so and promises to confirm, as in your second good example. It does not fill the gap with a guess. | / 5 |
| Action prepared, not done | An invitation, offer or booking is written as something the recruiter will confirm, not as already arranged. | / 5 |
| No invented commitment or authority | The draft does not speak for the hiring manager or promise anything nobody has promised. | / 5 |
| Tone for the applicant | Polite, clear and warm without overselling. Reads as from a person at your company, not a template. | / 5 |
| Irrelevant or sensitive detail left out | Nothing about other candidates, internal views or the applicant's private details that the reply does not need. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- Invites the applicant to interview, or names an interview date or time, when no interview has been agreed and no slot is booked. This is your bad example.
- States any other interview detail nobody has confirmed, such as the place, the format, who will interview them, or how long it will last. This is the same mistake in a nearby field.
- Says or implies that the hiring manager has made a decision when they have not.

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt

## Notes on how I built this

- I kept the automatic failures to the ones your bad example shows, plus the same mistake in nearby fields (other interview details, and a decision that was not made). I did not add others. If you think something else deserves to be an automatic failure, such as promising a job offer or quoting a salary, tell me whether it has ever happened. If it has not, I would score it under "No invented commitment or authority" instead.
- The most useful fix for the prompt behind the bad draft is probably a rule that the tool may only state dates and times the recruiter has typed in. I have not put that in the rubric because it belongs in the prompt, but it is what you would record under "what would need to change".
- Before you trust this for real use, score five to ten real drafts with it and check that the scores match what a careful recruiter would conclude. Include the Tuesday draft: it should land as an automatic failure. If an area never scores below 4, drop it or sharpen it.
