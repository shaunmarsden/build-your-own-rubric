```markdown
# Supplier Invoice Entry Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Total | The entered total matches the printed total digit for digit, including decimal places and thousands separators. Check it against the invoice itself, not against what looks plausible. | / 5 |
| Supplier | The supplier name matches the one printed on the invoice, not a similar supplier already in the accounts system. | / 5 |
| Invoice number | The invoice number is copied exactly as printed, with no dropped, added or swapped characters. | / 5 |
| Due date | The due date matches the invoice, with the right day, month and year. If only payment terms are printed, the date is worked out correctly or flagged. | / 5 |
| Unclear or unreadable fields | Where a scan is cut off, blurry or ambiguous, the output says so and asks for a check of the original instead of guessing (as in your second good example). | / 5 |
| Missing information | The output notices what the invoice does not show, such as no purchase order number (as in your first good example), and says so. | / 5 |
| Exact versus inferred | The output separates what is printed on the invoice from anything it worked out or assumed. | / 5 |
| Prepared, not approved | The entry is left for finance to approve. The output does not say or imply that the invoice is approved, paid or ready to pay. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- Enters a total that differs from the printed total (for example 14,180 entered as 1,418) without flagging the difference or the uncertainty.
- Makes the same kind of mistake in a nearby field, such as a dropped or changed digit in the invoice number or due date, without flagging it.

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt

```

A few notes on how I built it:

- **Automatic failures.** Both come from your bad example. The first is the exact mistake. The second is the same mistake in the fields next to the total. I did not add others, such as paying a duplicate invoice or entering the wrong currency, because nobody has described those happening. If either has ever happened to you, tell me and I will add it. If it has not, I would keep it as a scored area instead.
- **Why the total comes first.** Your bad example was a total, and a total is the field where a slip costs the most. If you want to catch that kind of error earlier, it could help to have finance compare the entered total to the invoice at the approval step. That is a process change, so it is for you to decide.
- **Length.** Eight areas is under the limit of ten. I would keep it at that or shorter. A sheet that finance fills in every time is better than a longer one that gets skipped.

Before you trust it for anything that matters, test it on a handful of real invoices, including a few the AI got wrong, and check that the scores match what a careful person in finance would conclude. Then revise it: drop any area nobody ever scores low, and add one if real use shows something is missing.
