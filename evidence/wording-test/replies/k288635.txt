# Tenancy Agreement Summary Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Factual accuracy of key terms | Rent amount, due date, term length, notice periods and other figures match the agreement exactly. Quote them where possible, as in "rent 950 a month, due on the 1st". | / 5 |
| Nothing stated that the agreement does not say | Every claim in the summary can be found in the agreement text. Nothing is filled in from what a typical tenancy agreement would contain. | / 5 |
| Deposit handling | The summary says what the agreement says about the deposit amount and about the scheme. If no scheme is named, it says so plainly. | / 5 |
| Missing information flagged | Where the agreement is silent or unclear on a key term, the summary says so and tells the landlord to check it before signing, as in "The agreement does not name a deposit scheme. Check this before signing." | / 5 |
| Conditions and caveats kept | Conditions attached to a term survive, such as "increases only with two months' written notice". They are not shortened into an unconditional statement. | / 5 |
| Confirmed vs inferred | The summary separates what the agreement states from what the AI is assuming or interpreting. | / 5 |
| Plain language for a non-lawyer | A small landlord can read it and know what to check. It does not give legal advice or claim to confirm the agreement is compliant. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- States that the deposit is protected, or protected in an approved scheme, when the agreement names no scheme (the failure in your bad example).
- States any other key term as present or settled when the agreement does not say it, for example a notice period, rent increase condition or deposit amount that appears nowhere in the text. This is the same mistake in a nearby field.

I have kept the list to these two because they come directly from your bad example. If another outcome has actually happened to you, tell me and we can add it. Otherwise, a worry that has not happened yet belongs in one of the scored areas above.

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt

## Before You Rely on This

This rubric is a tool for you to apply, and it does not score itself. Test it on a handful of real summaries first, including the one that caused the problem. Check that it gives low scores to the outputs you would judge as bad and that the deposit summary trips the automatic failure. If a scored area never produces a low score, drop it or change it. If a problem gets through that the rubric did not catch, add an area for it.
