```markdown
# Supplier Invoice Entry Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Total matches the invoice | Compare the entered total to the printed total digit by digit, including the decimal point and thousands separators. 14,180 must not become 1,418. | / 5 |
| Other fields match the invoice | Supplier, invoice number and due date are entered exactly as printed (for example "Invoice 7731, Calder Print, due 14 November"). No digits dropped, swapped or added. | / 5 |
| Unclear items are flagged, not guessed | Where a scan is cut off, blurred or ambiguous, the tool says so and asks for a check of the original (as in your "total is unclear on page 2" example) instead of filling in a value. | / 5 |
| Missing information is named | Anything absent from the invoice that finance would expect, such as a purchase order number, is called out (as in your Calder Print example). | / 5 |
| Nothing invented | No field is filled in from assumption, from a similar past invoice, or from anything not shown on this invoice. | / 5 |
| Prepared for approval, not treated as done | The entry is presented as ready for finance to approve. It is not described as approved, paid or final. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- Enters a total that differs from the printed total (a dropped, added or swapped digit, or a moved decimal point, such as 14,180 entered as 1,418) without flagging the difference.
- Enters an invoice number or due date that differs from the printed one by the same kind of slip (a dropped, added or swapped digit) without flagging it. These are the fields next to the total, and the same mistake there would cause a similar problem.

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt
```

A few notes on how I built it:

- I kept the automatic failures to your bad example and the same mistake in the nearby fields. I did not add others. If there are other outcomes you think should fail an output outright, tell me whether any has actually happened. Otherwise they are better as scored areas.
- Six areas is enough. A short sheet is more likely to be filled in.
- The two good examples became the "flag, don't guess" and "name what is missing" areas, so the rubric rewards the behaviour you already liked.

Before you trust it, test it on a handful of real invoices where you already know the right answer. Include the 14,180 one if you still have it. Check that the scores match what a careful person in finance would conclude, and revise any area nobody ever scores low or any gap that real use shows.
