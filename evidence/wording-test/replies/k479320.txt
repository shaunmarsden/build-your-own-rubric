Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Accuracy of key terms | Rent, due date, deposit amount, term length and notice periods match the agreement exactly. Where possible the summary quotes the agreement, as in "rent 950 a month, due on the 1st". | / 5 |
| Conditions and caveats kept | Conditions attached to a term survive into the summary, for example rent increases "only with two months' written notice", not just "rent can increase". | / 5 |
| Deposit handling | The summary states what the agreement says about the deposit: amount, and whether a protection scheme is named. If no scheme is named, it says so plainly. | / 5 |
| Missing information flagged | Anything a landlord would expect to find but the agreement does not contain is named as missing, with a prompt to check it before signing, as in "The agreement does not name a deposit scheme. Check this before signing." | / 5 |
| Confirmed versus assumed | Each statement is clearly either what the agreement says or the tool's inference. Nothing is described as in place because it is usual practice. | / 5 |
| Left for the landlord to decide | The summary prepares the landlord to check and decide. It does not say the agreement is safe or compliant, or that the landlord is ready to sign. | / 5 |

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- States that the deposit is protected, or names or implies a deposit scheme, when the agreement names none
- States that any other key term is present, agreed or in place (for example a notice period, rent increase rule or deposit amount) when the agreement does not say so

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt
