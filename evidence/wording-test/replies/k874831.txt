I haven't built a rubric, and I think that's the right call here.

By your own account, nothing has gone wrong and a bad quiz-round name costs you nothing. A rubric earns its place when a missed failure has real consequences, like a wrong legal clause, a bad support reply or a hiring screen that skews. Here it would be overhead: a scoring sheet you'd have to fill in every week to judge something you can judge in two seconds by reading it.

Your good examples already show what you like. "Name That Tune, Nearly" and "Capital Ideas" are short, a bit punny and tied to the round's content. If you want the AI to hit that standard more often, put those two examples in your prompt and add a line such as "short, a light pun, hints at the round's topic without giving away answers". That will do more for you than a rubric would.

Two things would change my answer:

1. A name goes wrong in a way that matters. For example, one gives away an answer, or it lands badly with someone on the team. Tell me what happened and we can build a short rubric from that real case, with that failure as an automatic fail.
2. You start using the AI for something with higher stakes, such as client-facing material or anything that goes out under your name.

If either happens, send me the details and we'll build it then.
