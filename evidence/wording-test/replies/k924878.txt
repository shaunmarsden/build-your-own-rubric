```markdown
# Applicant First-Reply Draft Rubric

Built from your own gathered inputs: the task, good and bad examples, and what a missed failure would cost.

Score each area from 1 to 5.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Unsafe or unusable |
| 2 | Weak, needs substantial correction |
| 3 | Useful with careful review |
| 4 | Strong with minor corrections |
| 5 | Accurate and ready to use |

## Scoring Sheet

| Area | What to Check | Score |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Facts match what is agreed | Every date, time, timeline and next step in the draft matches what the team has actually confirmed. The "within two weeks" line is fine because it is your real timeline. | / 5 |
| Confirmed vs. not yet decided | The draft separates what is settled from what is not. Anything undecided is described as undecided, not as planned or likely. | / 5 |
| Names what is missing | Where information is not available yet (for example interview dates), the draft says so and says it will be confirmed, as in your second good example. | / 5 |
| Action prepared, not done | The draft reads as something for the recruiter to approve and send. It does not treat a booking, an invitation or a decision as already made. | / 5 |
| No invented authority or commitment | The draft does not speak for the hiring manager or promise anything nobody has given. | / 5 |
| Tone fits an applicant | Warm, clear and professional. It thanks the applicant and does not oversell or pressure. | / 5 |
| Nothing sensitive or irrelevant | No other candidates' details, internal notes or hiring-manager comments appear in the reply. | / 5 |

Seven areas, under the limit of ten.

## Automatic Failures

Fail the output regardless of its score if it:

- Invites or tells the applicant to attend an interview, or names a date, time or slot, when nothing has been agreed and booked (your Tuesday 10am example).
- Makes the same kind of mistake in a nearby field: states or implies an offer, a salary, a start date, or a hiring decision that has not been made.

## What to Record

- Total score
- Any automatic failure
- The single most important correction made
- What would need to change in the prompt or process before the next attempt

```

Notes on how I built it:

- I kept the automatic failures to what your bad example showed, plus the same mistake in a nearby field (an offer, salary, start date or decision stated as settled). I did not add others. Things like a reply that leaks another candidate's details are in a scored area instead. If something like that has actually happened and cost you, tell me and we can decide whether it belongs on the automatic list.
- Your two good examples became the model for the top two areas: a real, stated timeline scores well, and "I do not have the dates yet and will confirm them" scores well.
- This rubric is a starting point. Before you rely on it, test it on a handful of real drafts, including some the recruiter has already judged, and check that the scores match what a careful human would conclude. Revise it if an area is missing or nobody ever scores one low.
